Personal finance advice is usually presented as a buffet: budgeting apps here, index funds there, a hot stock tip on the side. That framing is wrong. Personal finance is a **sequence** — each step only works because the ones before it are done. Doing them out of order isn't just suboptimal; it's how people end up investing in the stock market while carrying 24% APR credit card debt.


## The sequence

1. **Cover survival basics from current income.** Rent, food, utilities, minimum debt payments. Nothing else on this list is possible until this is stable.
1. **Capture every employer 401(k) match.** A 50% match is an instant 50% return. No investment on earth reliably beats that. If your employer matches, contributing up to the match is not investing — it's collecting pay you already earned.
1. **Kill high-interest debt.** Paying off a 20% APR balance is a guaranteed, tax-free 20% return. Compare that to the stock market's long-run ~7-10% nominal and the choice is arithmetic, not opinion.


## Why order beats intensity

A person saving $500/month while carrying $8,000 of credit card debt at 22% APR loses about $1,760/year to interest — more than 29% of everything they save. The same person who clears the debt first hasn't just saved that interest; they've converted their savings rate from leaking to compounding. **The order of operations is itself the biggest return available to most people**, bigger than any fund choice.

- Working through each step in depth: cash flow, emergency funds, index investing, allocation.
- Showing the arithmetic behind each recommendation, including when a common rule of thumb is wrong.

> The goal isn't to find clever investments. It's to stop doing the two or three things that quietly guarantee mediocre results.
