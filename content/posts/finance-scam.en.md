Twenty years ago it was penny stocks and forex seminars; today it's AI trading bots and crypto arbitrage funds. The products change; the architecture underneath doesn't. There are essentially **three structures**, and every pitch you'll ever receive is some combination of them.


## Structure one: impossible returns plus easy exits

Daily 0.5-1%, "monthly 10%," "guaranteed 25% a year." Here's the sanity check: the S&P 500's long-run average is around 10% *nominal* per year — with years of -20% and worse along the way (see [every major Nasdaq drawdown since 2000](/posts/finance-nasdaq-drawdown)). Anyone claiming to reliably beat that by multiples **while offering instant withdrawals** is describing a mechanism that cannot exist in regulated markets. The early withdrawals being smooth is not evidence of health; it's the design. Ponzi structures pay early redemptions promptly on purpose — that's what makes victims move their main capital in.


## Structure two: manufactured exclusivity

"Internal allocation," "invitation only," "my mentor's group." Legitimate investments are *marketed to everyone* — a fund that genuinely returns 30% a year raises billions and charges fees; it doesn't recruit strangers in group chats. The exclusivity serves a mechanical purpose: **it isolates you from outside opinions**. Everything you hear about the opportunity comes from people financially motivated to keep you in. The "teacher" or "mentor" persona is not a service; it's the delivery mechanism.


## Structure three: the threshold dressed up as a privilege

"You just need to reach accredited status and we can help you qualify." Real accredited-investor rules *restrict* what can be sold to you; they exist as protection, not as a door. Any scheme that actively helps you **over** a protective threshold in order to sell you something has told you, plainly, why the threshold was there.


## The one-question test

> If this opportunity were real, why am I the one being recruited? I have no special capital, no special information, no special skills the promoter needs. Think it through honestly and most pitches collapse on their own.


## If it's already happened

Act immediately: report to local law enforcement and your bank/card issuer (in the US, the [FTC scam alerts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/scam-alerts) track current patterns), preserve every transfer record, chat log, and app screenshot. Recovery odds drop steeply with time. And lose the shame fast — these operations are professional at exploiting exactly the reluctance to admit you were taken. The people who recover money are almost always the ones who reported within days.
